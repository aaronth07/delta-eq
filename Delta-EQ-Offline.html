Fit limits and method

Deterministic, bounded peaking-filter fit on equally spaced log frequencies. Q is limited to 0.3–6. Gain limits apply to each filter; overlapping filters can sum to a larger boost or cut. Existing filters are replaced; Undo restores them. No smoothing, psychoacoustic weighting or measurement-quality inference. Avoid boosting acoustic nulls. Target matching does not prove better sound.

How to use and interpretation limits
  1. Import a measurement and a target from the same measurement convention or fixture. TXT/CSV needs frequency in Hz and level in dB in its first two columns. REW headers and extra phase columns are tolerated. Comma, tab, space and semicolon delimiters are supported. With semicolons, decimal commas are supported.
  2. Choose normalization. A point anchor removes one scalar level offset from each curve; the band option averages dB at log-spaced points in the shared 100–10,000 Hz interval. Interpolation is linear in dB versus log frequency. There is no extrapolation.
  3. Adjust the target manually, fit or edit filters, then inspect the residual. The demo is synthetic and deliberately easy to fit. Real measurement noise, fixture variation, reseating and listening preferences are not modeled.
  4. Suggest headroom samples the combined EQ over DC to Nyquist and adds 1 dB. It is an estimate, not a true-peak or clipping guarantee. Exported settings affect sound only after you apply them in another app. Start at a low listening level.
  5. Save a session JSON to keep your curves and settings. Browser storage is a convenience and can be cleared. Private browsing or storage quotas may prevent it. No account, API key, upload or subscription is required for the offline copy.

Filter calculations follow Robert Bristow-Johnson’s Audio EQ Cookbook. Export syntax follows the Equalizer APO configuration reference. This is an independent tool, not affiliated with PEQdB or a reproduction of its model.
